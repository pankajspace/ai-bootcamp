3. Thinking Levels

A short settings tour. You look at the five effort levels and learn when each one is worth its cost, find the custom-instructions box where my token-saving trick lives, understand why we skip Ultra, and set a spend alert so your API bill can never surprise you.

Settings only≈ 5–10 minNo prompts to run

What you'll do

  • Open the Effort menu and read what each level is for. You stay on Medium.
  • Find Settings → Personalization → Custom instructions, the box where the “let Luna write the code” trick goes. You will look at it but not save anything yet.
  • Learn what Ultra does under the hood and why it is so expensive.
  • Set a monthly budget and an email alert on your OpenAI account, because you pay per token.

Before you start

  • You finished guide 2: permissions are on Approve for me and the model button reads 5.6 Sol Medium.
  • Keep the Codex app open. For step 4 you also need a browser and your OpenAI Platform login.
📘 Concept: reasoning tokens

When you give a model a hard task, it can “think out loud” to itself before it answers: break the problem down, consider options, check its own logic. That hidden thinking is made of reasoning tokens, and you pay for them exactly like the tokens in the visible answer. The effort level is simply how many reasoning tokens the model is allowed to spend. Light means “answer quickly, think a little”. Ultra means “take as long as you need, and bring help”. The art is matching the level to the task, because paying for deep thought on a trivial job is wasted money, and skimping on a hard bug is wasted time.

1

Look at the effort levels

Click the model button under the message box (it reads “5.6 Sol Medium ▾”) and hover over the Effort row. A list opens next to it. Don't change anything; just read the list and the small notes beside each level.

✓ You should see

Light, Medium, High, Extra High, Ultra. Ultra carries the note “Consumes usage limits faster”. The ✓ stays on Medium. Click anywhere outside the menu to close it.

💡 Tip

How I think about the levels:

  • Medium: what we use for this whole course. It is a good balance of quality and speed, and it is still sharp enough to find the tricky concurrency bugs we plant later in the course.
  • High / Extra High: what I reach for in my own day-to-day work when a problem is genuinely hard: a gnarly bug nobody can reproduce, or a design decision with many moving parts.
  • Light: fine for renaming things or answering a quick question about the code. I rarely use it for real changes.
  • More thinking is not always better. On a simple task, a high level makes the model overthink: it invents edge cases that do not exist and takes three times as long. Match the level to the task.
2

Find the custom-instructions box (the pro tip)

  1. Open Settings: click your name at the bottom-left → Settings, or press ⌘,.
  2. Click Personalization in the left list.
  3. Find Custom instructions: a big text box with a Save button at its top-right.
📘 Concept: custom instructions

Whatever you type in this box is silently attached to the start of every chat you open, in every project. It is the place for rules you never want to repeat: “always answer in short bullet points”, “prefer TypeScript”, or, as here, which model should do the heavy lifting. Project-specific rules go somewhere else (the AGENTS.md file, which we meet in guide 6); this box is for rules that apply to you everywhere.

My pro tip, for the day you are short on tokens, is to put this one sentence in that box. Read it now, but do not save it yet:

≡My custom instruction (pro tip, don't save it yet)Settings → Personalization → Custom instructions
All coding work should be done using GPT 5.6 Luna with Max thinking effort to save tokens.

Why it works: you keep Sol as your main model, so Sol does the thinking that matters, it plans the work and writes the tests. Then, because of this instruction, it hands the actual code-writing to Luna, the cheapest model, running at maximum effort. The tests act as guardrails: Luna's code has to make them pass, so the quality stays high even though the cheaper model typed it. You pay Sol prices only for the planning, and Luna prices for the bulk of the output.

📘 Concept: tests as guardrails

A test is a small program that runs your code and checks that it did the right thing, for example “placing a bid of 50 on an auction whose current bid is 60 must be rejected”. If a test fails, something is wrong. When a strong model writes the tests first and a weaker model writes the code to make them pass, the tests define what “correct” means, and the weaker model cannot quietly get it wrong without a red test telling you. We use this idea heavily from guide 11 onwards.

✓ You should see

A Personalization page with a Custom instructions box (its placeholder text starts with “Give ChatGPT extra instructions and context for all chats…”) and a Save button at the top-right of the box.

⚠ Heads up

Leave the box empty for now and click ← Back to app. If you save this sentence today, Codex hands all coding to Luna, and your results will not match what I describe in the “You should see” boxes of the later guides. Keep the trick for after the course, when you build your own projects and want to cut cost.

3

Know why you'll skip Ultra

Nothing to click in this step. I just want you to understand what the top level does, so you are never tempted to switch it on “because more must be better”.

📘 Concept: sub-agents

Ultra tells Codex to split the work across several sub-agents that run in parallel. Picture a team lead with four engineers: the lead (the main agent) reads your request, cuts it into independent pieces, hands one piece to each engineer, waits for them to finish, then merges their results and checks that everything fits together. Each of those engineers is a full model call reading the same project files, so the token bill is roughly multiplied by the number of agents, plus the lead's own work. This is great for a big job, say building three separate features at once in one go. For the single, focused tasks we do in this course, it is overkill: you would pay five times the tokens for the same result, and sometimes a worse one, because merging the pieces introduces its own mistakes.

✓ You should see

Effort stays on Medium. Ultra is never needed anywhere in this course.

💡 Tip

If you don't see Ultra in your Effort menu at all, that's fine. Some accounts and versions do not show it, and the course never uses it.

4

Set a spend alert for your API key

ChatGPT has monthly plans ($0 / $8 / $20 / $100) with a fixed usage allowance. You are not on any of those. You signed in with an API key, so you pay per token, and there is no built-in ceiling unless you set one. Let's set one now, before the build-heavy guides:

  1. Open the limits page below in your browser and sign in to your OpenAI Platform account.
  2. Set a monthly budget. When your spend reaches this amount, OpenAI stops accepting requests until next month. Pick a number you would be genuinely fine spending on learning.
  3. Set an email notification threshold, the alert. A good habit is to put it at about half the budget, so you get a warning while there is still room to finish what you are doing.
↗Open in browserChrome or Edge
https://platform.openai.com/settings/organization/limits
📘 Concept: budget vs alert

The two settings do different jobs. The budget is a hard stop: requests fail once you hit it, which protects your card but can also halt Codex in the middle of a task. The alert is only an email; nothing stops. Having both means you get warned early and, if you ignore the warning, you are still protected.

✓ You should see

Your budget and alert amounts are saved on the limits page. From now on, check platform.openai.com/usage after each build-heavy guide (7, 9, 10, 18 and 23 are the big ones) to see what it cost. You will quickly develop a feel for what a prompt costs.

💡 Tip

If that link doesn't open the right page, go to platform.openai.com → Settings (gear icon at the top-right) → Limits in the left menu. If you see a message about needing to be an organisation owner, you are probably logged in to someone else's organisation; switch to your own personal one from the organisation picker at the top-left.

Stuck or got a very different result?

If there is no Personalization page in your Settings, look under General for a “Custom instructions” box instead; older and newer versions move it around. If you cannot find it anywhere, skip step 2; the trick is for later and nothing in the course needs it.

If the Effort list shows slightly different names in your version, map them by position: the second-lowest level is our Medium. Keep the ✓ there.

Key takeaways

  • The thinking level is task-dependent. More thinking is not always better: high levels overthink simple tasks and cost more for no gain.
  • The course uses Sol + Medium throughout, for balance. In your own work, step up to High or Extra High only when the problem is genuinely hard.
  • Token-saving trick for later: let the strong model plan and write the tests, and let Luna at max effort write the code against them. Tests are the guardrail that keeps the quality up.
  • Ultra = several sub-agents working in parallel. Powerful for huge jobs, very token-hungry, never needed here.
  • With an API key there is no ceiling until you set one. A monthly budget plus an email alert at half of it is the minimum protection.
